Chapter 00 · The Architectural Paradigm Shift

The Sequential Recurrence Bottleneck

Before 2017, neural sequence processing was dominated by Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) and Long Short-Term Memory networks (LSTMs). These systems processed sequential tokens $x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n$ strictly left-to-right. To incorporate information from token $x_1$ into the calculation at token $x_n$, the signal had to traverse an uninterrupted chain of $n$ recurrent state updates:

$$h_t = \tanh(W_{hh} h_{t-1} + W_{xh} x_t), \quad \frac{\partial h_T}{\partial h_1} = \prod_{j=2}^T \frac{\partial h_j}{\partial h_{j-1}} = \prod_{j=2}^T \text{diag}(1 - h_j^2) W_{hh}^T$$
Equation 1.1: The Jacobian product across $T$ unrolled time-steps. When eigenvalues $\lambda < 1$, gradients vanish exponentially ($\lambda^T \to 0$); when $\lambda > 1$, gradients explode.

This sequential dependency imposed a double penalty: mathematically, distant relationships suffered catastrophic signal decay; computationally, GPUs were paralyzed because step $t+1$ could not begin until step $t$ completed.

Chapter 01 · Discrete Language Primitives

Subword Tokenization & Byte Fallback

Continuous neural networks cannot ingest raw alphanumeric strings. Character-level representations create sequences too long for quadratic attention ($n^2$), while word-level models require millions of vocabulary entries and still fail on unseen terminology.

Modern language models solve this trade-off via statistical subword compression algorithms—predominantly Byte Pair Encoding (BPE), WordPiece, and SentencePiece with Byte Fallback. Common words remain intact as single tokens, while rare words are fractured into recognizable morphological stems and affixes.

Chapter 02 · Continuous Geometric Representation

Vector Embedding Geometry & Cosine Proximity

Once text is converted into discrete integer IDs $t_i \in \{0, 1, \dots, V-1\}$, these identifiers index a lookup matrix $E \in \mathbb{R}^{V \times d_{\text{model}}}$. In this dense Euclidean space (typically $d_{\text{model}} = 4096$), semantic affinities correspond to geometric angles and directional offsets.

$$\cos(\theta) = \frac{\mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{v}}{\|\mathbf{u}\|_2 \|\mathbf{v}\|_2}, \quad \vec{v}_{\text{King}} - \vec{v}_{\text{Man}} + \vec{v}_{\text{Woman}} \approx \vec{v}_{\text{Queen}}$$
Equation 2.1: Cosine similarity and semantic linear parallelogram arithmetic in representation space.

Chapter 03 · Positional Relativity

Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE)

Because self-attention is permutation-equivariant ($\text{Attn}(P X) = P \text{Attn}(X)$), order information must be injected explicitly. While original Transformers used additive sinusoidal vectors ($x + p$), modern frontier architectures utilize Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE).

RoPE applies an orthogonal 2D rotation matrix $\mathbf{R}_{\Theta, m}$ to 2D chunks of the Query and Key vectors at token position $m$. The dot product $\langle \mathbf{R}_m \mathbf{q}, \mathbf{R}_n \mathbf{k} \rangle$ cancels absolute indices, preserving only the relative displacement $\Delta = n - m$:

$$\mathbf{R}_{\Theta, m}^{2D} = \begin{pmatrix} \cos(m\theta) & -\sin(m\theta) \\ \sin(m\theta) & \cos(m\theta) \end{pmatrix}, \quad \langle \mathbf{R}_m \mathbf{q}, \mathbf{R}_n \mathbf{k} \rangle = \mathbf{q}^T \mathbf{R}_m^T \mathbf{R}_n \mathbf{k} = \mathbf{q}^T \mathbf{R}_{n-m} \mathbf{k}$$
Equation 3.1: The orthogonality property of rotation matrices guarantees relative distance invariance.

Chapter 04 · The Mathematical Core

Scaled Dot-Product Attention & Variance Derivation

Self-attention maps Query representations against Key vectors to distribute probability mass across Value vectors:

$$\text{Attention}(Q, K, V) = \text{softmax}\left(\frac{Q K^T}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right) V$$
Equation 4.1: The canonical Scaled Dot-Product Attention formula (Vaswani et al., 2017).

Why is the scaling factor $\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_k}}$ required? Let $q_i, k_i \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1)$ be independent zero-mean unit-variance components. The dot product is a sum of $d_k$ independent random variables $Z = \sum_{i=1}^{d_k} q_i k_i$. By linearity of variance for independent variables:

$$\mathbb{E}[q_i k_i] = \mathbb{E}[q_i]\mathbb{E}[k_i] = 0$$ $$\text{Var}(q_i k_i) = \mathbb{E}[(q_i k_i)^2] - (\mathbb{E}[q_i k_i])^2 = \mathbb{E}[q_i^2]\mathbb{E}[k_i^2] - 0 = (1)(1) = 1$$ $$\text{Var}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d_k} q_i k_i\right) = \sum_{i=1}^{d_k} \text{Var}(q_i k_i) = d_k \implies \sigma = \sqrt{d_k}$$
Equation 4.2: Full algebraic proof showing dot-product variance scales linearly with vector dimensionality $d_k$.

Without dividing by $\sqrt{d_k}$, logits scale to large magnitudes ($\pm 3\sqrt{d_k}$). The softmax function saturates into a one-hot distribution with near-zero derivatives, triggering catastrophic vanishing gradients during backpropagation.

Chapter 05 · Inference Memory Engineering

Multi-Head, Multi-Query & Grouped-Query Attention

Multi-Head Attention (MHA) projects queries, keys, and values into $h$ subspaces, allowing the model to jointly attend to information from different representation aspects at different positions.

During autoregressive inference, previously generated Key and Value vectors must be cached in GPU high-bandwidth memory (HBM) to avoid recomputation. In long-context inference ($128\text{k}$ tokens), standard MHA's KV-cache consumes dozens of gigabytes per concurrent user, creating severe memory-bandwidth starvation.

$$\text{KV Cache Bytes} = 2 \times 2 \times n_{\text{layers}} \times n_{\text{kv\_heads}} \times d_{\text{head}} \times s \times b$$
Equation 5.1: Memory consumption where 2 accounts for FP16 precision, the second 2 for Key and Value tensors, $s$ is sequence length, and $b$ is batch size.

Chapter 06 · Associative Memory Banks

Feed-Forward Networks & SwiGLU Gating

While self-attention facilitates token-to-token message passing, the position-wise Feed-Forward Network (FFN) functions as an associative key-value memory bank storing factual knowledge acquired during pretraining. It expands representations to $4d_{\text{model}}$ before compressing back.

$$\text{SwiGLU}(x) = \left(\text{Swish}(x W_1) \otimes x W_3\right) W_2 = \left((x W_1 \cdot \sigma(\beta x W_1)) \otimes x W_3\right) W_2$$
Equation 6.1: The SwiGLU gated activation mechanism (Shazeer, 2020) standard in modern architectures.

Chapter 07 · Gradient Optimization

Residual Gradient Highways & RMSNorm Optimization

Residual skip connections create unobstructed identity pathways $\frac{\partial (x + F(x))}{\partial x} = I + \frac{\partial F(x)}{\partial x}$, preventing gradient attenuation across hundreds of layers. In modern systems, Pre-LN has replaced Post-LN to eliminate warmup instability.

Furthermore, Root Mean Square Normalization (RMSNorm) replaces traditional LayerNorm by demonstrating that the mean-centering step is mathematically redundant for training stability:

$$\text{RMSNorm}(x) = \frac{x}{\text{RMS}(x)} \odot \gamma, \quad \text{where } \text{RMS}(x) = \sqrt{\frac{1}{d} \sum_{i=1}^d x_i^2 + \epsilon}$$
Equation 7.1: RMSNorm eliminates mean calculation $\mu$, cutting GPU kernel memory passes by ~50%.
Plate 1.8 LayerNorm vs RMSNorm Vector Walkthrough

For vector $x = [2.0, -1.0, 3.0, 0.0]$ with $d = 4$:

  • LayerNorm: $\mu = \frac{2 - 1 + 3 + 0}{4} = 1.0 \to \sigma^2 = \frac{1+4+4+1}{4} = 2.5 \to \frac{x - 1.0}{\sqrt{2.5 + \epsilon}}$ (Requires 2 full synchronization passes across GPU threads).
  • RMSNorm: $\text{RMS}(x) = \sqrt{\frac{4 + 1 + 9 + 0}{4}} = \sqrt{3.5} \approx 1.87 \to \frac{x}{1.87}$ (Executed in 1 fused pass with zero mean synchronization).
Chapter 08 · Generation Dynamics

Autoregressive Generation & Temperature Dynamics

During inference, models emit predictions token-by-token. Logits $z_i$ from the final linear projection are modulated by sampling temperature $\tau$ prior to multinomial selection:

$$p_i = \frac{\exp(z_i / \tau)}{\sum_j \exp(z_j / \tau)}$$
Equation 8.1: Temperature scaling. As $\tau \to 0$, distribution converges to greedy argmax; as $\tau \to \infty$, distribution approaches uniform noise.

Chapter 09 · Hardware & Modality Scaling

Frontier Innovations: FlashAttention, MoE & ViT

Three foundational hardware and architectural breakthroughs govern modern production frontier deployments:

Plate 1.10 SRAM Memory Hierarchy, Dynamic Routing & Spatial Patchification

1. FlashAttention (IO-Aware SRAM Tiling)

Standard attention requires writing the $N \times N$ attention matrix to High-Bandwidth Memory (HBM, 3 TB/s). FlashAttention tiles blocks into on-chip SRAM (19 TB/s) by fusing the kernel and tracking running softmax statistics via online rescaling, yielding a 2×–4× end-to-end wall-clock speedup.

2. Mixture of Experts (MoE)

Rather than activating all weights for every token, MoE routes each token dynamically to the top $k$ of $N$ feed-forward expert networks via a softmax gating router $G(x) = \text{TopK}(\text{Softmax}(x W_g))$. Parameter capacity scales to hundreds of billions while per-token FLOPs remain constant.

3. Vision Transformers (ViT)

Extends self-attention to computer vision without convolutional inductors. Images $x \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times C}$ are divided into $P \times P$ patches, flattened into $N = \frac{HW}{P^2}$ vectors, and processed as standard sequence tokens.
